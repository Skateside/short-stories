My name is Senka Klarić and I have to break into a warehouse to steal a sword that’s “unnaturally dark and feels cold to the touch.” I’ve been ordered to do this by someone representing Baron Mihael, the Wolf of Bezcodru. Failure to take the sword will result in them drinking away my little sister, Ljubica, as our payment of the blood tax. That sword must have incredible value – it’s very rare for any vampire to be willing to give up prey for an item, let alone a wolven fzeg.

The sword isn’t the only mystery about this whole ordeal. The warehouse is built on an island that’s so square, it might be artificial. There are some windows on the second floor, but the walls are too smooth to climb. Realistically, the only way in or out is a bridge that goes from the port to the door. Foreign sailors prize it for its security and the fact that the two guards at the start of the bridge are mortal, not vampires – it’s one of the things that alerts the locals that the warehouse is suspicious. The other thing is the emblem on the wall. Usually, buildings in the town of Usta have an engraving of a goblet to represent the Crimson Court or a wolf’s head to represent Mihael. This warehouse shows a bird holding a fish and a laurel – no-one locally recognises it.

Usta itself was built on the mouth of the river (hence the name: “Usta” means “mouth” and yes, every girl has heard the same joke). The river flows from Bezcodru’s capital, but it flows through the dense Stillborn Forest with unknown horrors dwelling within. (Ljubica used to say that there was a powerful fairy living there and that she protected the trees, which is why there were so many. I think she was making it up, but I was never daft enough to find out.) To avoid that journey, sailors leave their goods in warehouses here so the locals can take them the rest of the way, whether we wanted to or not. Most warehouses are on land, in or around the port. They’re locked when not in use and rarely guarded privately – something else that makes this warehouse stand out.

The bridge from the port to the warehouse is sturdy. It’s wide enough for a hand cart to go in both directions and a couple of dozen feet long. The warehouse is only open during the time of loud bells, which isn’t for another 3 hours; during quiet bells, the warehouse is closed and the bridge guards tell people to wait. This gives me the perfect chance to sneak in, I just need to get onto the bridge without the guards noticing. There’s a support in the middle of the bridge – my best bet for sneaking in is to climb that.

I’m glad that I’m not the kind of girl who cares about her nails – climbing this support means digging my fingers into cracks between stones and finding footholds to boost myself up. Even if I did care, I’m not pretty enough to make a living by looking good. Sometimes I wish I was. The guards always turn to watch the local prostitutes as they walk by, but my chest won’t fill out their dresses the same way. Some girls don’t even need that, their faces seem to be enough to get the same response; mine never has. A girl that pretty could probably just walk on by with the promise of a kiss, maybe more, or at least negotiate a way out of being caught. As for me … well, plan A is “don’t get caught.”

Suddenly one of my feet slips. Stones crumble and fall into the water below. My heart races as my foot desperately tries to find purchase, adrenaline pumping through my legs, making them shaky. As I grip on to the support, I hear a voice from above ask “Did you hear that?” and I freeze. I think I can survive the fall, but I don’t want to check. I hold my position. I hear shuffling coming from the guards’ location. I wait and wait. My arms ache, I hold my body as close to the support as possible. A voice responds to the first one: “hear what?” I realise that there’s only been a couple of seconds between the two voices. A sighing grunt from the first voice lets me breathe again. Onwards and upwards.

When I make it to the top, my arms are tired and my feet feel a little numb, but I haul myself over the barrier and step onto the bridge, just as the local chapel chimes its quiet bell. I can’t remember who the chapel is dedicated to – it’s not a god, the gods are dead, it’s an arch seraph, but I still can’t remember who it is. Something about sacrifice, I think? The sigil on the door looks like a naked man carrying a wheel on his shoulders – maybe it’s about sacrificing your clothes? Apparently they offer healing if you donate enough. It doesn’t matter, the nine chimes help disguise my footsteps as I get to the warehouse door. The first loud bell usually wakes everyone up and I have to get the sword back to the Court before then.

The warehouse door is large, but there’s a person-sized door built into it. I press my ear to the door, my eyes looking towards the guards at the other end, just in case. I hear nothing moving inside. I can’t see a lock on the door so I test the handle – the door opens and I slip inside, silently. Well, silently until I close the door behind me – it’s on some kind of weighted hinge and it bangs as it closes. Maybe the echo in here makes it sound louder but I feel like it was loud enough to wake the dead.

Even though it’s always night in Ostoya, it’s dark in here. I can make out some shapes while my eyes are still adjusting, my ears are burning as I try to listen to everything. I jump over a row of solid crates that are as tall as my waist and press my body up against them, tilting my head as I listen towards the door. As I feared, it opens and the light from Usta seeps in. The light reveals some stairs against the wall I’m facing – might be worth checking when I can, there might be a clue to the sword’s location, but I daren’t move yet.

Waiting is the worst. I’m trying to calm my breathing, my heart’s pounding so loudly that I’m amazed that no-one can hear it. If I reach for my dagger, whoever opened the door will hear it; if I don’t, I might be unarmed in a fight. The figure at the door stays there. Looking around, perhaps? Eventually – finally – they leave, closing the door behind them, with the same bang. It’s muffled, but I hear one of the voices from before say something like “I told you.” I feel my entire body release its tension. I sit here for maybe half a minute, until I feel like I could move without my legs giving way beneath me. Feeling my bravery return, I head for those stairs.

The stairs are solid, there’s a door to the room, and that door is locked. I take this as a good sign – it means that there’s something in here that needs protecting, even from the guards. The lock picks and dagger that I brought along with me slip easily into the lock. It’s careful work, all done by touch, but I’ve got time. Carefully … carefully … I turn the lock and the door gives way, silent as the grave.

The upstairs room is just as dark as the rest of the warehouse. I can’t get complacent – the locked room in the secure warehouse could well have some other protective measures. I get low to the ground and brush my fingers over the floor, feeling for anything that would trip a would-be thief. I can’t feel anything obvious and I can’t see any shapes that look dangerous. Better – as I scan the room, a shape on (what I assume is) a table against the wall looks like a stand holding a sword. The sword being in the first place I check? Normally it’s not that easy.

I creep up to the sword, looking around as best I can and listening out intently. Hearing nothing, I get to the stand – a sword is there. It looks dark, but the room is dark; I touch it and it feels about the same temperature as the rest of the room. Is this the sword I want? It might be a useful weapon if I need it, could even be worth something given its prominence, but I don’t think I can leave yet. As I take the sword, my heart stops. The stand drops, held stable by the weight of the sword. The door I came through slams shut. A voice from the darkness yells “Who’s there?” So much for plan A.

I only have a couple of seconds to identify whoever called out before a blinding light activates. My eyes, used to the previous darkness in the room, squint while I adjust before I can see them. Male dwarf (can I offer blood?) mortal mouth (can I offer sex?) armed and swinging a blade in my direction (nope – gotta fight). I clutch the sword that I’ve just taken and use it to knock away his blade. He looks back towards me and I realise that this is a proper fight – either I will drop him or he will drop me. I’m looking at him, trying to see whatever advantage I can. As a dwarf, he’s shorter than me, but not so much that I can stay out of his reach; my reaction time seems like it’s a little faster, so bladed combat might actually be my solution.

He swings his sword at me again – wide and low. I’m able to step to the side of it, using my sword to deflect his. He swings at me again, heavily (in a way that would probably favour a larger weapon, like a hammer or a two-handed axe) up towards my head. I’m able to duck underneath it and slash at his stomach. It bounces off armour that he’s wearing and doesn’t seem to do any damage. With my new position, my sword catches the light – out of the corner of my eye, I can see it reflecting back, better than most swords I’ve ever held. This contrasts with the sword he’s holding, which looks like a shadow is being cast on it even though it’s in the light. I think he’s got the sword I’m looking for, so I’ll have to take him out and claim it from him. Just my luck that it would be protected.

Me looking at his sword and working out my situation leaves me distracted. His next slash cuts my upper arm. I’m not wearing armour, it would have been too heavy to wear while climbing, so the blade cuts through my top and easily injures my arm. This isn’t my first sword fight. True, they’re not my best skill, which is why I stick to sneaking, but I’ve been cut before. This cut, however, hurts more than any other I’ve had. It’s as if the wound pierces deep into my bones without having to physically get there. I think I even scream in response, but I have to put that out of my mind. I’m in a fight – I have to be ready for his next attack.

His next attack isn’t what I’m expecting – he doesn’t swing his sword, instead he raises his off hand and says something in a language that I don’t recognise. In the split second that I have to try and understand what’s happening, I feel an intense burning all over my skin – almost a chemical burn rather than fire. I feel slightly nauseous, my skin feels irritated, my body feels pain. Was that magic?

My curiosity doesn’t last long. He takes sand out from a pouch on his belt, says something else in that strange language, and blows it in my face. As it connects, my eyelids immediately feel heavy. I’m not able to focus my eyes, my limbs become limp, and I collapse.

As I wake up, I try to move my arms only to find that they’re shackled. My feet are as well. My eyes open and I realise that I’m sitting on a chair with my arms behind me. I’m slightly relieved that I’m still wearing clothes – that’s not always guaranteed.

“Ah, she’s awake.” It’s the same voice as earlier. The accent is odd, I’ve not heard it before. He continues: “you’ve got guts, trying to steal from a Company warehouse. You made it past the guards and up to this room – you even got our silvered blade. What are they paying you for it?”

Silvered blade? I’ve heard of them, never seen one. Well, not until a few moments ago. At least, I think it was a few moments, I don’t know how long I was out (I don’t see a clock so I’ll have to wait for the bells). Some say that silver is good against vampires, some say it’s no better than any other blade. I’ve never wanted to get close enough to find out – I pay my blood tax by filling a vial. If it _is_ effective against them, I’m amazed that they haven’t come in here to seise it. Unless … he was sleeping here – does he live here? Does that make this warehouse a residence? Could that prevent a vampire entering – is that how it works? Is that why they sent me to get the sword?

“You don’t feel like talking. That’s a shame, but it’s not necessary.” He puts a sadistic grin on his face – the sort of grin that Ljubica used to have when she’d taken something of mine. She could be such a brat. I hope she’s OK.

The dwarf takes a copper piece out of his pouch (I think it’s the same pouch he took the sand from) and shows it to me with that same devilish grin. It’s square with a hole through it and a design on it that I think is supposed to be a mountain? It’s an Ostoyan penny, I’ve seen and spent many of them in my time. He lays it flat in his hand and makes a strange gesture above it with his other hand. He says something in that weird language he used before as the penny starts to glow. This must be magic. I’ve never seen it before, I thought only the vampires could use it. Now I’m worried – is he a vampire and he’s managed to hide it from me? Is he connected to the Crimson Court? If not, how have they allowed him to continue – they tell us that magic is theirs by right. I’d love to say that my face isn’t betraying my fear, but he’s casting magic.

A beam of light slowly grows out of the penny and moves towards my face. I desperately struggle against my bindings; I’m still shackled. I turn my face but I can’t look away. This light – this weird, organic, magical light, it’s coming towards me and there’s nothing I can do. I brace myself, unsure of what I’m up against, as it reaches my head.

Nothing changes. I’m unharmed so far. I’m still in the room. Still shackled, but I doubt that his spell would have changed that. I look back at him and … it’s as if I can feel him inside my head. It feels like a memory of a time that I knew him. What even is this?

“You’re scared of magic and you want to leave.” He speaks that aloud. Does the spell empower him to state the obvious? “You’re here because of someone. A girl. Your sister?” My mind sharpens immediately – how could he know that? “You don’t know what this spell does. That’s OK, I’ll show you.”

Immediately, my head feels like it’s being squeezed, like his essence is pushing against the walls of my mind. I feel like I’ve got my back against a door, using all my strength to keep the horde out – and that horde is him. He’s like an ooze slipping between the window panes of my memory, a fog flowing under the door to my thoughts. I grunt and writhe as I try to keep him out, keep my head’s house free from him. It doesn’t hurt, but it feels so uncomfortable that it may as well hurt. My eyes are closed yet all I can see is him.

As quickly as that started, it stops. My head drops as I no longer need to tense. I’m out of breath and I can feel a tear rolling down my cheek. As I look up at him, he has a look on his face that’s … I don’t know, surprise? Respect? Sadism? He looks back at me and says “you’re strong.”

He starts walking towards me. I struggle against the shackles again, they stubbornly remain attached. I don’t like where this is going – I like it even less when I can see that dark sword in his hand. He lets the blade drop onto my leg and he gently slides it up my body – no attempt to harm me yet. I know that he’s playing with me, I know that he’s just trying to scare me. Unfortunately, it’s working. He presses the blade against my cheek. It’s cold, colder than it should be. I need it to save my sister, I don’t know if he knows that; he’s not acting like it if he does.

“It seems that you can resist magic, not many can. But you’re a victim of your own success. You wouldn’t talk willingly and we couldn’t do this painlessly. That leaves me with just one option.” He gets close enough for me to knee him in the junk – sadly, the shackles don’t let me. “Who sent you?” Our eyes lock and my mind tells him to bleed out in an alley way. I don’t know if he heard me or he just reads my facial expression but he pulls the sword away from my face at just the right angle to cut me.

The pain is excruciating. I can feel the wound drill down through muscle and bone. The underside of my eyeball hurts, my tongue tingles, my teeth ache. I clench my jaw to take my mind off the sensation. I can feel the air around my face, the cut must be trivially small, but the pain – the pain is more than I’ve ever felt before. I hope he’s not in my head because I don’t think I can take many more of those cuts.

There must be a way out of here. My hands flail against the shackles themselves, hoping that I can feel something to help me. I feel the ring of metal around my wrists, I feel the way they pinch together on one end, I feel the pin that must go through a loop which stops the shackles opening – and then I feel that I can move that pin. It’s not secured in place. Why wouldn’t he do that? Unless … he didn’t have time – the spell didn’t make me sleep for long. I haven’t heard the bells, but I wouldn’t have if I was only asleep for a minute or two. I could confirm it if I could see my feet, I just need to angle my body correctly.

Oh, _bleed me dry!_ Did he see my relief? He’s coming back towards me, sword raised, stern look on his face. He puts the sword against my cheek again – the left cheek, same as last time. I grit my teeth – I know that I really won’t enjoy this. “I’ll ask a second time, I won’t ask a third – who sent you? Was it your sister? Who is she with?” I’ll kill him for talking about her – slowly if he says her name!

All my fury, all my defiance, all my clarity of thought, it lasts right up until he cuts me again. Then everything is pain. Searing. Anguish. Torment. I scream through a clenched jaw, I’d find the sound interesting in any other situation – especially if someone else was making it. Tears roll down my cheeks, I don’t bother to try and stop them. If I can get them out of my eyes, I’ll be able to see better. The pain makes my brain spin and I can think of nothing else, but the room slowly comes back to me. My cheek is aflame but I’ve regained my resolve. I remember Ljubica, I remember the sword, and crucially, I remember the pin.

I push the pin up with my fingers. It’s not enough – the pin is awkward – but I can keep trying. All I need is time. Time that would be far easier to spend if a magical dwarf with his magical sword wasn’t standing in the same room, looking at me like I owe him something.

He comes at me again, sword raised again. He looks angry. I need time. “No! No – please. I-I’ll talk.” I forgot that I had a voice, I guess the screaming reminded me. I’ve seen this before, although I was on the other end of it. A victim sounds scared, they sound like answering questions costs them a part of themselves. But I’m Ostoyan, living costs me part of myself (the blood tax) and foreigners never understand that. His accent is odd, he’s not from the Empire, he won’t realise what I’m doing. Indeed, his face changes to a patronising smile.

“Well then,” he says with an undeservedly victorious tone. “Who sent you?”

I flick the pin, it falls back into place, I need more time. “The … that … that girl you saw,” flick, fall. “M-my sister,” forgive me, Ljubica. Flick, fall. “She’s … she’s with the Crimson Court,” flick, fall. Oh – defeated victims feel shame, they lower their head, I lower mine. Flick, fall, but slower than before. “I-I was sent by the Crimson Court.” That shouldn’t be news to someone in his position. The Crimson Court are the vampires that rule the Ostoyan Empire, Bezcodru is an imperial province, and Baron Mihael is a member. This is a gamble – he can use magic and I’ve only ever known the Court to do that. If he’s connected, he’ll ask me questions that I don’t think I can answer. But this warehouse doesn’t feel vampire-owned – the emblem on the warehouse wall isn’t anything I recognise. I think the odds are in my favour. Flick, slow fall.

“And what does the Court want with us?”

He’s heard of them but he’s not with them. Flick, wait, fall – next time. “They want, they want me … t-to get …” I whisper, too quietly to be heard, keeping in character: “your sword.” I keep my head down, I want to draw him close. Close enough to bite.

“… to get what?” He takes a step closer.

I’ve got him, but I need to get him closer, so I keep whispering. “Your sword.”

He takes another step closer to me. I can feel anger seeping into his words. “What do they want?” He takes another step closer to me. I keep my head down – I’ll be able to see his feet once he’s close enough to me. This might not be wise, I need my vision, but he’s the kind of man who’d respond to this: I start crying.

He takes one final step towards me. I flick the pin with all my might and it doesn’t fall. The shackles open and my left arm is free. I don’t care if he realises what’s happening, I swing my still-shackled right arm directly at his head. Dwarves have thick skulls, but that’s still got to hurt. He staggers backwards, giving me time to look down at my feet. Of course the pin is near my left leg, he’s right-handed, and it’s also just a pin – I pull it out and free my feet.

I grab my arm shackles, keeping my right arm bound (although freely moving) – he’s armed and I’m not, I need something that can defend myself against his sword and I need that immediately. He swings it towards my head, but that shackle bar deflects it, putting me in the best position possible to hit him in the face. As he recoils, I look around the room, still lit from before, and spot the silvered sword – I rush towards it, letting my shackles drop.

I grab the sword and turn back to face him, the odds evened – a poor choice, on reflection. His blade hits my face and the pain is beyond description. Everything goes blurry, my rational thoughts almost stop. This is far worse than anything he’s done. I can’t see … but … he’s armoured – but his face isn’t. I swing in his direction, vertically and wildly – hopefully too wildly to counter. Two or three swings later, my blade hits something and I hear him staggering backwards. I open my eyes as much as I can and aim my sword swings at his face.

I’m blood-drunk, I know that, but this is the closest I’ve come to an advantage since I got here. I have to rely on instinct, my vision isn’t working. I slash again towards his face and connect. The footsteps he makes give me a direction to aim towards – I kick towards his knees to knock him down. Luck is on my side and that blurry mass was a dwarf – a dwarf that’s now on one knee. I kick him as hard as I can in the back of his head, which hits the ground with a very satisfying thud.

Bloodied and defiant, I stand over him, angle the silvered sword, and push it down towards him as hard as I can. Does he sense it coming? Do I just miss his head? Maybe he moves out of the way a little bit? My sword hits him, but I think it pierces his shoulder, pinning him to the ground, face-down. He cries out in pain. I look at him, only able to open my right eye. I try, but my left eye won’t open at all. I put my hand to my face and realise that my left eye is nothing but a mess of fluids. Looking back at him, through only one eye, I scream in his direction as loudly as I can – he hasn’t beaten me, I’ve beaten him!

I stand up, but I’m woozy. The blood loss, the pain, it’s catching up to me. I’m trying to look around the floor – the dwarf had a blade, a blade that I need (and I’ll kill the entire bloody Court if it’s the wrong blade). Where is it? I can barely see … I look for the blade-ish shadow. My left eye is gone … it’s gone … my right eye is OK and I can make out the shape of the sword. I lean down to grab it, my balance mostly holding together – holding together long enough for me to grab the sword. The hilt must be wrapped up in something, it feels like a normal temperature. I make my way to the door and hear the dwarf mutter something in that magical language he used. I turn towards him and push myself away to avoid a magical attack that he launches with his pinned hand. He’s failed!

The door downstairs slams open and I realise that the guards outside would have heard my scream, even from the other end of the bridge. Stealth is pointless, they know I’m here (even if they don’t know where specifically). This sword is great, but I can only wield it so well. My best bet: run. Run as fast as I can, with everything I’ve got left. I grip the sword as tightly as my hands will allow and run down the stairs.

One guard is waiting at the door, I don’t know where the other is. He has a spear drawn and thrusts it in my direction as I get closer. I’m just instinct covered in skin at this point – I side-step the spear well enough to slash at his chest with the sword. He’s wearing armour, isn’t he? Guards wear armour. It seems to hurt him. Is that the sword? I don’t stop for long enough to find out, I swipe at his neck and he drops like a sack of potatoes. Pushing his body out of the way, the bridge is in front of me.

I start running along the bridge. I hear someone shout behind me, a local accent, but I keep running. I’ve got Ljubica’s freedom in my hand, nothing will stop me getting her back, not now. As I run, I suddenly feel my leg explode from underneath me. I drop down onto my knees as I see it: the other voice came from a guard, who threw their spear into my leg. I turn to see the guard coming my way, I try to move, but this leg won’t let me. I look around and retrieve the sword that I dropped when I was struck, and slash it in the guard’s direction. It barely connects with him, but it’s enough to push him backwards. I slash again, this time at his legs – that drops him down to my height. From my prone position, I slash again, this time vertically. It connects with the guard’s helmet, but seems to go through it. Lifeless, the guard falls. I pry the sword back from out of his head and use it to cut away at the spear that’s lodged in my leg. The shaft breaks – that’ll have to do.

Everything hurts, everything is distorted. My leg screams in agony and my face stings. I’m losing blood. I can barely see, but I hear the chapel bells start to chime, announcing that it’s ten quiet. That’s a direction to head in. I don’t need much, just my leg to stop hurting. Then I can make it to Mihael’s proxy, I can get my sister back. I have to limp on a bloody leg all the way there.

I make it to the chapel door, the naked guy carrying a wheel embossed on the wall. By this stage, I’m sweating, I’m dizzy, and it’s taking the last of my strength to push open the doors. I’ll find a way to donate – blood tax, physical labour tax, sex tax, whatever they’ll take. The doors are heavy, but they give way. As I step into the chapel I see … nothing, no-one, darkness … it’s empty. Unable to stand any more, I collapse and pass out. I’m sorry, Ljubica.

I feel myself waking up. I stand, without any pain. It’s bright here – light everywhere. Ostoya’s always dark so this light is unusual, but it’s not blinding. Is this what it’s like to live under the sun? It’s a big flaming ball in the sky, it heats the ground and can burn exposed skin, so I always assumed that it would just set everything on fire. I can even see a glow across the sea on clear days. But this isn’t hot, it’s comfortable.

Still trying to work out where I am, I hear a man’s voice. The voice is kind, but sounds fatigued – like a father after working the fields. “Not many choose to die in one of my temples – you’ve got my attention.”

“I’m dead?” I reply.

“You’re dying. You’re not supposed to run on a leg that badly wounded.”

“I can’t die. I have to hand over this sword to get back my sister. I don’t even know what this sword is.”

The voice solidifies into a figure. Male, naked, carrying a wheel, tired face. “It’s shadowsteel. A fiendish material, created when someone infuses part of a soul into the metal. Very dangerous, difficult to destroy, and used when casting the darkest of magic.”

“Is that why Mihael wants it?” The question goes unanswered – maybe this guy doesn’t know? “He’s got my sister, he’ll eat her alive, but he’ll trade her for this sword. I have to get it to him.”

“He might kill you when you hand it over.”

I don’t care. “But Ljubica will be safe, she can get away while he’s on me.”

That seems to resonate with him. “You would sacrifice your life for hers?”

“Yes. She’s my sister.”

“You can’t walk, you can’t stand, you’re barely breathing. You’ve lost too much blood.”

“Then heal me. Please.”

He pauses before answering. “I can restore your body, but there will be a cost. Your grip on this moral plain would be weakened, the heavens would claim you before you become this close to dying again. Your life would no longer be your own, it would be mine. You would be my champion and it’s a lot of work.”

“I agree …” I hesitate as something dawns on me. “Wait – would I have to sacrifice my clothing?”

He laughs. “Only if you want to.”

“Then I agree.”

His voice becomes reassuring. “Stand tall, Senka Klarić, your sacrifice has made you strong. As people pray to me to avoid misfortune, I grant you an eye that can see where it should go,” he rubs my eye. “As you fell in sacrifice, I grant you wings to soar in service,” he touches my shoulder blades. “I name you Champion of Morael, arch seraph of the fallen goddess Vetara. Rest in her love.”

Darkness. Sweat. No pain, not any more. I’m back in the chapel. I stand before I remember my leg, but I stand tall – my leg is healed. I put my hand to my face – my eye is healed as well. I feel stronger, even if I can’t explain why, but I have no time to question it – my sister’s still in danger and I hurry across town to our meeting point.

I could knock, that would be polite, but I’ve had a rough night. I push the door open. Inside are three men dressed garishly, one more so than the other. Tied to a chair, I see Ljubica. She’s clearly been crying but her face lights up when she sees me. The more garishly dressed of the men speaks. “Ah – loyalty to your pack – I admire that.” I realise that I’m talking to Baron Mihael. I didn’t recognise him, he’s usually in his wolven form. I bow my head. I’ve got no love for this guy, but I’ve come so far that I don’t want to lose everything now due to a lack of propriety. He continues: “and you have the sword – hand it over.”

“First give me my sister!”

There’s a pause as I fear I may have overstepped, but it’s too late to back down now. Mihael laughs. He laughs again. As he laughs a third time, the other men join in. He looks over to one of them and nods, that one cuts Ljubica free and she rushes over to me. We hug. “Thank you, sis,” she whispers. As we look at each other, her expression changes to curiosity or concern. “Your eye?”

“I’ll tell you later.”

A beautiful moment ruined as Mihael pipes up. “Touching. The sword.” He stretches out his hand.

I’ve got my sister. I don’t know what they want with this blade, but a deal’s a deal. I hand it to him, hilt first. As he takes it, I hear a fatherly voice in my head. “Don’t worry, Champion, we’ll stop him yet.”
