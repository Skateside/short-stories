I lower my body back down to the ground, my wings disappear as soon as I get there. I’m spent, my eyesight is acting up, my back hurts – more so from the wings flapping. I try to walk towards the konoba but my body is exhausted. I collapse.

When I wake up, I find myself in a bed. The room smells sweet, but slightly spicy. Someone’s sitting on the edge of the bed. I try to move and that catches the person’s attention. They turn to face me and I see that it’s Mateja – she must have changed into sleeping clothes, this must be her room. She breathes a sigh of relief. “You’re alive, I was worried. You were out for a couple of hours.”

I want to say something really impressive right now, but all that comes out is “yeah.”

“And you can fly?!”

“Morael gave me wings, still trying to work out why.”

“They saved me, I think that’s a pretty good reason.”

I look to the window and see fire burning outside. I try to see, but my back hurts too much to sit up. Mateja notices and fills me in.

“Tonka. Plus everything we could find from the gnome – sorry, the fzeglaich. The fall should have killed it, but Madam Iva wanted to be sure.”

“You’re not down there?”

“I said my good-byes before they started the fire. Dimitar’s last act before Madam Iva made him leave.”

“They followed him then?”

“Maybe. Madam Iva thinks so. The vampire turning to mist is how they escape. He’ll take a while to recover, but it means this house won’t be safe for much longer. If she has to relocate, she doesn’t want Dimitar to know where they’re going. We’ll probably all be safe until at least first loud.”

“Are you staying with her?”

Mateja lowers her head, clearly thinking. “I don’t think so.” She looks towards me. “I don’t think I could go through something like this again, I’m not strong like you, I don’t think I’d fit in the Order.”

I want to say something comforting, something like “you’re strong” or “you’ve got other skills” or something like that, but she speaks up before I can.

“By the way,” she puts the cheekiest grin on her face. “This was a terrible date – I don’t think it’s going to work out between us.”

Playing back, I stubbornly pull the covers up. “Hey, I paid for this!” We both laugh, but laughing hurts. She’s immediately sympathetic.

“Get some rest, I think we’ll feel better afterwards.” She gets under the covers and closes her eyes.
