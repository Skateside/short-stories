I’ve been caught before, but this time is different. Returning the item can sometimes remove the punishment, offering a vampire blood or a mortal sex can sometimes reduce it, but it’s rare that the target responds the way he does.

“You may keep it if you help me.”

Long robes and a deep hood made him look like someone trying to stay out of sight. The dirt makes it look like he usually sleeps outside (although the smell doesn’t). When he asked for a room, the owner told him that a bed would cost 5 silver, a meal would be 2, and a bath would be a further 3 – 10 silver (or 1 gold) in total. He paid it with neither complaint nor hesitation – an alley-sleeper spending someone else’s money. A warm bath would relax him, the locked door would make him feel safe, the bed would help him sleep. I waited for 2 more quiet bells after he’d gone up the stairs to make sure he’d be resting, I heard nothing when I listened through the door, and I silently picked the lock. I don’t know what gave me away but he spotted me with my hand on his purse.

“Help you do what?” I’m hungry. Most of the food that I find usually goes to Ljubica. I need a good couple of meals to help my back recover and he looked like an easy mark. His purse feels like it’s got between 3 and 5 gold in it. Perhaps as much as 10, depending on the exact coins. That would keep me and my sister fed for a fair few days. I don’t mind helping for a decent reward, but I need to know that it’s worth it.

He sits up. His purse was in his clothes, I knew that he’d be naked, but his body isn’t what I’d expect. Alley-sleepers are usually scrawny, often scarred, he looks like he’s in good shape. A soldier or a guard maybe? “My friend is trapped and I need help freeing him. Help from someone like you.”

“You mean a thief? If I was a good thief, you wouldn’t have spotted me.” I’m still holding his purse, I still need to know what I’m getting into before agreeing.

Even in this dark light, I can see his eyes examining me. Scanning every part of me. I’m armed and armoured – he must have spotted that – but he doesn’t seem afraid. Then he gives me a look that makes me realise I’ve got a tell, I just don’t know what it is. “You would not have been able to stay hidden from me.”

“Why not?”

“Because I can see you,” he gets out of bed. No scars, no visible disease. “All of you.” I’m looking at his naked body while I’m fully clothed and wearing a veil-mask but he can see all of me? “We recognise our own – I am surprised that you do not see me.” Is he on something? He’s standing right in front of me, of course I can see him. Recognising our own suggests that he’s also a thief, which would explain the money and the lack of street smell.

I still need to know about the job. Not many holding cells in Bezcodru; when vampires are the law, being drained is often the punishment. “Where is your friend?”

“A house on the far end of town.” He turns and points. I could attack him now – why is he so trusting? “I believe the road is known as Carrion Lane.” Nothing but ruins on that road. Apparently one of the houses is haunted. “If you are willing to help me, we would need to go now.”

Trusting but smart – if we were going at first loud, I’d slip away before then – but that’s a very unusual combination. It usually means that he’s part of a larger organisation and that he has men planted in this place; it means that I’m already in his trap and the only way out would be through. The job doesn’t sound too dangerous (yet) so this is probably my best available choice. “OK.”

He takes a step towards me – I immediately straighten up and reach for my dagger. He sees the reaction and speaks, still as calm as ever.

“You are standing beside my clothes. Or would you prefer to walk the streets next to a naked man?” That would be funny, but it would draw attention – for this job, that’s probably unwise. I go to move towards the door to get out of his way but he stops me. “Forgive me but I do not trust you yet.” He reaches out his hand for his purse. Reluctantly, I hand it over. He then gestures to me to wait next to the bed so he’s between me and the door. I do so, but I make sure that he can see my hand resting on my dagger’s hilt.

He gets dressed in front of me. It’s not unpleasant, I’ve certainly seen worse looking men. He doesn’t watch me. I don’t understand this man at all – I’m ready to defend myself from him throwing me onto this bed, he doesn’t even want to try. When he’s dressed, he looks at me and says. “We depart.”

“You got a name?”

“Aloysius.” That is the single stupidest name I’ve ever heard in my life. “Do you have a name and do you have a face?”

Fine. I lower my veil. “Senka.”

“I am pleased to meet you, Senka.” He bows slightly but stretches his hand out towards the door, offering me the chance to go first so he can keep an eye on me. How kind.

We walk down the stairs, out of the building, and towards Carrion Lane. The conversation on the way is just as strange.

“I could not help but notice the care with which you descended the stairs. Are you in pain?”

“Do you care?”

“Of course. I would not have asked if I did not care.”

“Why do you care?”

“You are assisting me. It would not do to repay such an action with apathy.”

I don’t know how much I trust him. Do I tell him that a fzeglaich lacerated my back? Or that a guard threw a spear through my leg and that I died in a chapel. The silence is starting to feel like it might get awkward, I guess I have to say something. “I was attacked. It’s healing. I’m fine.”

“I am sorry to hear that. I often forget how dangerous this place can be.”

One more set of quiet bells ring before we get to our destination. When we get there, I can’t help but give Aloysius the side-eye.

“You friend is trapped in here?” I point to the dilapidated house with the semi-attached door that we’re standing in front of, the one that looks like it was abandoned before I was born.

“Yes.” He says it in such a matter-of-fact way. “I can not enter the property yet, you must go first.”

The street is empty so an ambush must be waiting inside. This is a lot of effort to go to just to punish me for trying to take some money. Finally I ask “what’s waiting for me in there?”

“Regrettably, I do not know.” With that tone, I think I actually believe him.

I step forward and walk up to the door. I take the time to knock and ask aloud if anyone’s home. A face sees me. Female. Wild hair. Burning eyes. Sharp teeth. Floating. I can see through her. She shrieks at me “STAY AWAY FROM HIM!” I fall backwards. Dead gods, ghost are real?!

Aloysius calmly walks towards the house as I turn to start running away. “I still need your assistance.”

I don’t know why I bothered to stop and explain myself. Fear-stricken, I point at the house. “That’s a …” I can’t even say the word.

“I can heal your back.”

I … I don’t know what to say. I haven’t moved since he said it. Eventually, out comes “w-what?”

“Your back is wounded, that fall must have hurt. I will heal your back if you continue to help me.”

I’m still standing there, my arm is still pointing at the house, my face has barely changed. I’ll admit it, it would be nice if my back stopped hurting.

“Do … do you know who she is – was?”

“No,” he looks back at the house and then back at me. “I believe that my friend is still in there, but I can’t enter. You must take the lead, I shall follow immediately afterwards.”

He wants me to walk into a haunted house? Wait a moment … “why can’t you enter?”

“I am being prevented by a powerful ward.” He walks up to the door, the ghost doesn’t show. He stands there, just like a vampire does. Have I been tricked into coming here with a vampire? If the ghost lives there, would that make it enough of a residence to keep a vampire out? Or maybe the friend is doing that?

“Maybe there’s another entrance?” With a house this size, there might be a back door for easy access to a garden. If the ghost is guarding the front door, she might not be at the back one?

Aloysius agrees to let me check, although he comes with me. The grounds are overgrown, no-one’s tended them for years. The house is just as worn down on the back, but there is a door. I gesture to Aloysius to wait as I creep up to it. I listen closely to it. I can hear my heart beating and my heavy breath, but I can’t hear any footsteps … which I guess I wouldn’t because the ghost floats. Very carefully, I look though the door’s window. Nothing is moving. I put my hand on the door handle. I know I have to turn it and that delaying won’t help, but I still don’t want to. I take a couple of breaths to stead my nerves and slowly turn the handle. It’s not locked. That means we’re going to go inside.

The air out here is still. Nothing is moving, nothing wants to be here. I enter the building. The air in here is stagnant but as soon as I clear the door, it’s as if a gentle breeze picks up in the house. Dust moves by itself and something feels different, even if I can’t describe it. True to his word, Aloysius follows in just behind me. We’re inside for a second or so before I see her. She’s coming straight towards me.

“WHAT HAVE YOU DONE? GET OUT!”

She picks up a vase or something, throws it straight at me. I dodge just in time – it crashes against the frame of the door we came through.

“STAY AWAY FROM HIM!”

She raises her hands as if to push me out of the way. She’s at the other end of the hallway, but I feel a sudden thrust against me. She’s pushing me away, even from over there! The surprise of all of it knocks me back.

“GET OUT!”

Another ghostly shove pushes me out of the house. I watch as her terrifying face rushes towards me. As it gets to the house’s door, she stops. I’m lying on the floor trying to wrap my head around this. My armour is pointless, would my dagger even work? I look upwards into a genuinely haunted house to see her face staring back at me. After a few seconds, she turns her face to Aloysius. She screams at him – her hair bellows as if being blown by a wind. She pushes him against the wall and shrieks at him as she shrieked at me.

“GET OUT!”

She turns to grab something (I can’t see what from here) and Aloysius ducks out of the house. As he does, she immediately calms down and vanishes away. Aloysius checks to make sure that I’m alright before saying the sort of thing that only he could say. “Did any of that seem strange to you?”

OK, I’ll play. “Which part?”

“She only noticed me after you had left.”

I only just found out that ghosts exist, how was I supposed to know that bit’s strange? “Some kind of possessive girlfriend?”

Aloysius looks at me as if he doesn’t understand the concept I’ve just mentioned. “The idea that love should be limited, that love can cause pain, it is a devilish corruption so far reaching that it would make me weep were I to focus on it.”

He says the strangest things. “A lot of love going around where you’re from?”

“We were all giving and receiving love at all times. There were no laws, only love.”

It felt a lot more like being used than being loved when it happened to me. Sure, it kept a roof my head – and Ljubica’s – but I was glad to get out before she was old enough to pay that same tax. While reminiscing on that, I suddenly remember something. “You said that you couldn’t enter, but you could?”

“The ward lifted as soon as you entered. I do not believe that was a coincidence. Indeed, I believe that it is a necessary first step in rescuing my friend.”

“Let me guess,” I take a deep breath and swallow, hard. “Step 2 would be getting rid of that ghost?”

“You have guessed correctly.” He studies the house, I don’t want to say anything because I don’t want him to say that we’re going back in there. “She seems unable to leave the property. I suspect that her remains are still inside. Burying them with proper rites may put her spirit to rest. Perhaps we could learn what has happened?”

“How do we do that?”

“We search.” Of course. “Well, I search, you distract her.”

What?! “How?”

“Your presence seems to be enough. Staying inside and avoiding her attacks should give me plenty of time to search the building.”

I … I really don’t want to. My lower lip even wobbles at the thought of re-entering. Aloysius seems to notice.

“You are scared of her?”

I have to think about that. I _am_ scared of her, but it takes me a moment to work out why. “I’ve killed before. When they die, they’re dead. They’re just … not there any more.” I look into the house. The ghost isn’t visible, but I know she’s there, I just know it. “Is she what happens when we die?”

“Only if something goes wrong.”

I squeeze my eyes tightly closed, but when I open them, the house is still there. I take a long and tremulous breath. I can do this. I’ve gone through houses without being seen before. I’ve survived attacks before. I look around, we’re still standing outside. Delaying still isn’t helping. Would talking? “A-any ideas where you will start searching?”

“I will start at the top and work my way down, although I must be careful. She keeps referring to a ‘him’ and I suspect that she means my friend, but I have not seen any signs of movement other than hers. He may be upstairs.”

If I can keep him talking, maybe he’ll forget about going inside? “Who is your friend?”

“Ignatius,” another really weird name. “We fought together.” OK, probably more of a soldier than a guard. Would explain why he’s going this far – that “war buddies” bond is only second to “family.”

Please keep talking. “How will I know that you’ve found her remains?”

“I suspect she will react.” He looks at me, I think he’s seen through my feeble deception. “Are you ready?”

I look up at him, shakily. “N-no.” Another deep breath. “You can really heal my back?”

“Yes.” He says it so calmly that I believe him completely. “I will also give you the purse that I promised earlier.”

Good meal. Warm bath. Pain-free back. OK, I’m ready. “I need you to open the door. I’ll run through, as deep into the house as I can. That should keep her distracted.”

Much to my misfortune, he agrees. I lower myself, bending my knees to get a sprinting start. My back hurts, but not enough to stop me. As Aloysius opens the door, I start running. The ghost’s face fades in as I get to the door. She screams at me as I run through her. I dart into a room near the end of the hallway and put my back to the wall just inside the door. I hear Aloysius’ brisk footsteps. At least he hasn’t left me here. 

Ghosts go through walls! Her face comes through the wall. She screams at me again. “GET OUT!” She keeps moving into the room as I run out of the door and across the hallway. I hear something moving in the air and instinctively duck. A book hits the wall in front of me. I seem to be in a kitchen – I grab a pot and throw it at her. No, I don’t know what I expected – it goes straight through her and she doesn’t even notice.

The dagger! It stopped the fzeglaich healing. Could it hurt her? She’s coming my way – I unsheathe my blade and hope. I slash at her, I don’t know if I hit her or not. She seems surprised but then very angry. She raises her hands up to me and shoves me with that same ghostly blast from before. I get pushed against the wall, my back hitting it. Aloysius, if you’re lying to me, I will kill you!

She’s still coming towards me. I scramble back up onto my feet and evasively get around her as she wails “GET OUT!” I run into another room. Looks like a store room or larder? Not wise, nowhere to go. I sheathe my dagger while I can. As she comes in, she raises her hands up to give me another blast. I manage to duck out of the way. Old tins go flying. She’s blocking my way out, but I can run through her. I close my eyes as I do and crash into the wall outside the room afterwards. I run into a third room. Looks like a drawing room. “STAY AWAY FROM HIM!” I guess this room is closer to “him” than the others.

As I ready myself to dodge her next attack, I hear a crashing noise upstairs. Even she looks up. “NO! DON’T TOUCH ME!” That’s a new phrase, is that the reaction that Aloysius said might happen? As soon as she sees me again, she’s back to shrieking “GET OUT!”

I dodge another flying object that she throws my way. Aloysius runs into the hallway carrying a bundle of something. After him is … some kind of beast? It stumbles down but stops in the hallway and turns to look at me. There’s a brief moment of pause as we see each other. It’s some weird cross between several creatures – a lizard’s tongue, a rat’s nose, goats eyes, scales. It looks to the ghost before lunging towards me. The ghost shrieks again “STAY AWAY FROM HIM!” Is this that “him?”

The “him” is clumsy but physical, I can avoid him – I can attack him if I need to. The way he comes for me, though. His arms are out-stretched, his eyes are wide. It’s like he wants to eat me? I’m able to evade his grasping as the ghost screams at me “STAY AWAY FROM HIM!” This time she almost sounds desperate? It’s an oddity that I’ll worry about when I’m outside. I run through the hallway as fast as I can. As soon as I’m outside, I turn around and re-draw my dagger. That beast runs towards the door but stops when he gets to it, bellowing or squalling. He can’t leave, some magic force keeps him in. His cry seems almost mournful? As I look over to Aloysius, hoping that he’ll reassure me that’s not Ignatius, I remember that he’s holding a bundle.

“Is that a body, or is that just bones?”

“It is just bones. I do not know bones well enough to determine the sex, nor how long ago the body died, but I am hopeful that these belong the to ghost. Tell me, did she react at all?”

“She said something different – ‘no, don’t touch me.’”

“I take this as a good sign. It suggests that the bones are hers indeed. I am unfamiliar with the exact funereal rites of Ostoyans but typically a body must be buried with kinds words said over the deceased. Do the rites here differ significantly?”

I don’t actually know, I’ve never been to a funeral. I might have caused a couple. “Probably not.” I hope that answer’s correct.

“She seems bound to this house but unhappy, I do not believe that she would enjoy being buried facing it.” He looks around while I wonder how much a dead person would care about such things. He nods towards a tree. “That tree would shield her view and the earth would remember her.”

We walk over to the tree, wild and gnarled. It looks like this might be a _maslina_ – it grows olives. Easy access to that might explain the big house. As we take the remains to the tree, I get a sinking feeling as I ask “did you bring a shovel?”

“No, I did not anticipate needing one.” He places the remain down with the tenderness of a mother putting a baby down to sleep. “We must use our hands but the grave does not need to be deep, it need only cover the bones.” I’m really glad that I’m not the sort of girl who cares about her nails.

We’re digging for ages. I heard a local chapel ring its quiet bell seven times shortly after we started. I’m tired by the end. If Aloysius is as well, he does a good job hiding it. He does a less good job of hiding that wound on his arm – it looks painful but he hasn’t mentioned it. He unwraps the skeleton and together we place the bones down where they look about right. The chapel bells ring eight quiet by the time we’re done, so that took an hour. We stand over our newly created grave looking slightly solemn.

“I did not know this body in life, but I hope that death can provide her the rest that she deserves.” He leans over to me as if prompting me to say something.

I think _you scared the piss out of me and made me question everything I understood about my place in the world_ is probably not appropriate so I go with “She was … formidable … unrelenting. Whatever it was that she cared about, she cared deeply. I can respect that.” I’ve never been good with words.

We push all the soil we dug up on top of her, Aloysius stands back up, solemnly again, and says something in a language I don’t recognise (and yet it sounds familiar) that seems like the sort of thing that someone should say at a time like this. I turn to him to ask what we should do next when I gasp. The ghost – she’s here! But, less scary: her clothes are tidy, her hair is neat. She’s still terrifying, but she’s looking down at the grave.

“I thought I would reside in that house evermore.” Her cadence sounds slightly archaic. “He brought me to this house. Further to that,” she places her hand on her neck. Yeah, I’ve been choked as well, I don’t understand what men enjoy about it. “As my life faded, I cursed him without knowing it. ‘All should see you as the beast you are. Lust without love.”

“He deserves it.”

Aloysius corrects me. “He did, but now he deserves a chance at atonement. Perhaps even redemption.”

“And how do you plan on doing that?”

“We lift the curse by going against what it contains. ‘No woman will spend the quiet with you’ – as a woman, you must willing spend the quiet with him.”

Please tell me that I misheard that. “You want me to _fuck_ him?!”

Can I even say anything to make this guy reconsider spending 4 hours with that thing? “He raped and killed a woman! Why should we help him at all?”

I’m lost for words. I want to ask if he knew this would happen, if this is why he needed me, but I don’t think it would do me any good. At least, not now. This leaves me with just 1 question. “What can we use for rope?”

“‘May?’” He shrugs – probably the most normal thing I’ve seen him do all day. “If we go back in there, he’ll probably attack you to get to me. I will defend myself.” I put my hand on my dagger’s hilt. “You know that, right?”

Try not to kill him – I’ll do my best. I’m not looking forward to going back in, but at least that creature is something that I can actually fight. Aloysius confirms. “yes. In our first encounter, I was cut by one of Ignatius’ claws.”

“You said that you could heal my back. Could you heal your arm?”

“Yes. But I cannot do both without resting. If I healed my arm, I would break a promise that I made to you.” He turns to look at me. “If you give me your word that you will remain here, I can make good on that promise now.”

He would stay injured just to keep a promise? I don’t understand this guy at all, but I nod and turn my back to him. I’ve come this far and I’m slightly curious about what’s going to happen. “I am afraid that there may be some scarring. Healing scars requires more power than I have access to at this time.”

“That’s fine, I’m covered in scars. Thank you.” As I turn back, he hands me the purse as well. Weighs the same. I thank him again. So that’s what Morael did to me.

We talk a bit more while we wait, there’s not much else to do. When we hear the first loud bell, the body on the bed starts writhing again. It makes a sound that slowly becomes more human. Aloysius goes to release him. Although I’m tired, I jump up and draw my dagger. If this thing is still a beast then I’m going – I did my part, I got paid, the job’s done!

The creature within the sheets is, indeed, a man. Haggard, wispy, confused. I think I was expecting some kind of explosion when the curse lifted? It just ended. He speaks with a strained voice, like an old man. “Wh-what happened?”

Aloysius responds. “Ignatius, a woman cursed you, another has lifted that curse.” Ignatius looks at me. My dagger’s still raised. “This must be the first day of your atonement, you have much for which you must make amends. I will help you but I will not do it for you.”

He looks at me, I try not to grimace too much. “I thank you.” He looks at Aloysius. “Both of you.”

The moment is touching, I guess, but I don’t want to stay here. I still don’t like that guy. I say my good byes and leave to get some sleep. Ljubica is never going to believe this one!
